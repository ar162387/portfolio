## Primary offer: AI receptionist

**Buyer problem:** Routine calls arrive while staff are busy or outside the agreed coverage hours.

**Proposed scope to validate:**

- Answer inbound calls during agreed overflow or after-hours coverage.
- Answer routine questions using approved business information.
- Capture the caller's details and reason for calling.
- Book an eligible appointment using available slots and agreed rules, or take a booking request if direct integration is unavailable.
- Pass appropriate requests to staff and provide an agreed summary.

**Required before offering it as ready:** A working telephone demo; confirmed calendar/CRM compatibility; tests for unavailable slots, cancellation rules, uncertainty and human handoff. A demonstration should label sample calls and sample customer records clearly.

**Delivery structure:** Understand the call flow; build a scoped demo; test with the owner; agree acceptance criteria and support; launch the agreed coverage. Setup and ongoing operation can be priced separately after costs and scope are established. Usage limits and ongoing responsibilities must be explicit.

**Proposed primary website action:** Hear a receptionist demo. Use a recording or telephone demo once available. Until then, use “Request a receptionist demo” and deliver that request honestly.

## Secondary offer: Custom business software

**Buyer problem:** Enquiries, appointments and invoices are scattered across tools or spreadsheets.

**Proposed scope:** A tailored system for lead capture and status, customer records, appointment management and invoicing. Start with the workflow the business actually needs. Define any payment, reminder, migration and reporting integrations separately.

Offer this to software buyers in its own right and to receptionist clients when the need emerges. Connect to an adequate existing CRM/calendar rather than making a custom rebuild a condition of purchasing the receptionist.

**Proposed action:** Discuss your booking workflow.

## Plain-language terms and one customer journey

- **Positioning:** Who you help, what problem you solve and why they should consider you.
- **Offer:** The specific job you will do, what is included, how success is checked and the commercial terms.
- **One-liner:** One sentence explaining your offer.
- **Three-liner:** A short explanation of the problem, solution and next step.
- **CTA:** The next action you ask a reader or viewer to take.
- **Soft CTA:** A small step such as hearing a sample call or requesting a checklist.
- **Hard CTA:** A direct commercial step such as booking a demo to discuss their business.
- **Lead:** Someone who may buy and has provided an appropriate way to continue the conversation.
- **Conversion:** The particular action being measured, such as a demo request or a signed client.

Proposed journey: relevant Reel or thoughtful outreach → profile with the same offer → receptionist demo page → qualified enquiry → demonstration and discovery → scoped proposal → client.

Use one next action in each post. Match its destination to the promise. A receptionist post should lead to the receptionist offer, not an unrelated general portfolio page.

## Outreach and measurement

Draft outreach around a real observation or a relevant question. Never claim a business missed a call unless that call actually happened. A voicemail test is an observation, not evidence of lost revenue or purchase intent. No outreach has been sent.

Measure conversations with suitable owners, demo requests, attended demos, proposals and clients. Reel views alone cannot show whether the new positioning is working. After a pilot, gather actual call handling, qualified enquiries, confirmed bookings, handoff performance and operating costs before publishing result claims.
