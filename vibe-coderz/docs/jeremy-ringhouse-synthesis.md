# Voice-agent agency: research synthesis and revised plan

Prepared 14 October 2026 for Shah / Vibecoderzz / @vibecoderzai. Research and planning only. This document supersedes the earlier bio, homepage copy and proposed industry-selection requirement. It does not authorize publication or claim the demos have been inspected.

## The decision

Specialize in AI voice agents. Remain open across Home Services and Health & Beauty in the USA, UK and Australia. Select campaign examples for relevance, then let buyer conversations, delivery effort and retained clients reveal where deeper specialization makes sense.

Custom CRM, booking and invoicing software remains available, with a separate scope and buying decision. Voice should carry the main brand story. A working existing calendar or CRM should usually be connected rather than replaced.

The strongest proposed point of view is: **the business owner has work to do; the call still deserves proper attention.** Show what happens to the caller and to the owner afterward. Make the ongoing responsibility visible, rather than presenting setup as the whole service.

## How the sources fit together

Saad's early-stage openness and Jeremy's product focus can coexist. One addresses who you may serve; the other addresses what you repeatedly deliver. Our application is a focused voice service across several industries, with contextual examples and explicit acceptance tests.

There is a real tension with custom software. Jeremy deliberately avoids adding unrelated services. For us, secondary software should earn its place through the same enquiry-to-work journey, or be sold as a clearly scoped project. It should not become a mandatory bundle or expand the agency back into a general menu.

The [Lawnline example](https://lawnline.marketing/services/complete-solutions/) remains useful for coherence: a recognizable buyer, one connected story, credible people and meaningful packaging. Its industry restriction and bundled marketing delivery are not requirements for this agency. Ringhouse adds a second lesson: demonstrate how the system behaves. Together they suggest coherent positioning plus visible evidence, rather than generic service cards with new names.

## Three creative directions to develop

These are original working concepts, not final copy or proposed trademarked product names. Each needs three example posts and a website first-screen study before selecting the final expression.

| Direction | Problem and promise | Proposed visual | Editorial strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **You're needed elsewhere.** Recommended | The person doing the work cannot always give callers attention. Show coverage built around that reality. | Founder or staff busy with work alongside the call and resulting lead/appointment. | Human, warm and adaptable to both market families. |
| **Hear it for yourself.** | Owners may reasonably distrust an AI receptionist. Let them test what it actually does. | A playable call, a difficult question and a visible outcome. | Demonstration supplies the argument; fewer vague claims. |
| **The work after hello.** | A pleasant call is insufficient if nobody can act on it. | Call details become a useful summary, calendar action or customer record. | Connects voice with software without forcing a bundle. |

Use the first direction as the main story, supported by the second as proof and the third as the software bridge. Avoid using all three as competing slogans in the hero.

Possible recurring editorial series:

- **Call My Bluff:** Shah challenges a demo with one difficult but realistic caller request. The payoff is the exact handling, including a limitation when it appears.
- **After the Beep:** Explore a recognizable unanswered-call situation and a useful alternative. Use observed facts or labelled roleplay, not invented lost-revenue statistics.
- **Then What?:** Follow the call into the workflow. Did the appointment actually land? Did staff receive enough information? What still needs a person?

The bio should make four things apparent: Shah's identity, a reason to watch his work, the business relevance, and one useful next step. Geography can live elsewhere if it crowds out meaning. The earlier generic bio is not the final recommendation.

## Strengthened service and proof plan

Before final sales copy, catalogue the founder's existing demos. For each, record the business scenario, telephone/web access, tested actions, integration, remaining limitations and shareable footage. Existing business-specific demos are founder-reported; inspecting them is still required.

Build the service around this proposed sequence:

1. **Understand the calls:** coverage hours, routine questions, appointment rules, escalation and staff responsibilities.
2. **Make a relevant demonstration:** use a scenario the owner recognizes, with accurate business information or clearly labelled sample data.
3. **Test together:** include unavailable times, caller corrections, unknown answers, human handoff and integration failure. Define which outcomes constitute acceptance.
4. **Launch the agreed scope:** coverage and supported actions must match what was tested.
5. **Review and improve:** specify who reviews calls, how often, how changes are approved and how incidents reach the owner. Promise only a support process we can actually resource.

Give a busy owner the next useful action first. Detailed logs remain accessible for someone who wants them. Show this distinction in the website and demonstrations before presenting a complex dashboard as the main benefit.

Price only after including telephony, models, hosting, integration work, review/support time, payment costs and acquisition effort. Jeremy's minute-cost illustration is not our price floor or a net-profit forecast. Software builds need their own estimate and support responsibilities.

A scoped, jointly tested launch is a more credible early commitment than guaranteed recovered revenue. Outcome guarantees, if ever offered, require a measured baseline, clear exclusions and delivery evidence.

## Acquisition and learning plan

Screen prospects by recognizable call pressure, reachability of the decision-maker, useful recurring questions, value of the next action, compatibility and delivery effort. Apply the same screen across both market families. A clinic with a manager may need a different introduction; Jeremy's nail-salon anecdote is not a reason to reject the entire sector.

Run small, comparable campaign batches with relevant examples. Track the route: original contact, demo experience, requested follow-up, attended conversation, proposal, signed client and subsequent operating performance. A demonstration should provide an explicit way to request contact; do not silently treat captured caller ID as a sales opt-in.

In sales discovery, establish what currently happens and what the owner wants changed. Calculate financial value only from their own inputs, distinguishing a captured enquiry from a sale. Use a tailored demonstration to test fit, then a scoped proposal. Do not copy another founder's outreach volume or assume ads are proven.

Measure content against our own baseline: retention, profile visits, relevant questions, requested demos and qualified conversations. Measure service delivery separately: captured intent, confirmed actions, handoff usefulness, failures, support effort and retention. Review patterns after the first month; a permanent vertical decision needs consistent commercial and delivery evidence, not one popular Reel.
